CHARM Facility

A unique irradiation facility

CHARM is a unique irradiation infrastructure at CERN, long used for testing the electronic equipment deployed in CERN's accelerators — and accessible to external users. It reproduces three key radiation effects:

Aalto Activities · Publications

Adaptive non-repetitive LiDAR–inertial odometry

Non-Repetitive Scan Correction for LiDAR Odometry via Spatial Analysis in CERN Facilities

2026 — Accepted

NRLIO: Adaptive Non-Repetitive LiDAR–Inertial Odometry

Continuing from the previous work, NRLIO tightly fuses LiDAR data with an IMU, adaptively choosing the best matching strategy for each scan and validating updates with a statistical check. Tested across four different sensors and environments, NRLIO outperforms leading systems — especially on narrow-view and sparse sensors — while running in real time on a standard computer.

Appendix · A2

CHARM intervention with CHARMBot

  • The CHARM radiation-testing facility requires a camera stream to teleoperate a platform that moves electronic devices in and out of a radiation zone
  • The camera stream is provided by a robot called CHARMBot
  • The current GUI only provides 2D visual information, making depth and spatial relationships hard to perceive
  • As a result, operation time increases and operators lack a comprehensive view of the scene
Appendix · A3

A 3D GUI for CHARM teleoperation

  • Developed a 3D GUI to enhance teleoperation capabilities
  • Integrated a 3D map generated from a 3D LiDAR and camera system
  • The interface provides better spatial awareness, reducing operation time and improving efficiency in radiation-zone operations
Appendix · A4

3D mapping with non-repetitive LiDAR

  • Autonomous operation of mobile robots in large-scale, feature-sparse environments (like CERN's facilities) poses significant localization and mapping challenges
  • Non-repetitive scanning LiDAR makes traditional registration methods unreliable
  • Conventional techniques (e.g. generalized ICP) are optimized for repetitive scan patterns and often fail on sparse or irregular data with limited overlap
